# Mountain Biking in Adventure Racing

<br/>

## Introduction

Mountain biking is a core discipline in adventure racing, covering distance efficiently while requiring navigation, team coordination, and equipment management. This guide covers bike selection, setup, pacing strategies, team dynamics, and mechanical repairs for adventure racers.

<br/>

## How Biking Works in Adventure Races

Expect varied terrain (singletrack, gravel, technical sections), navigation while riding, team pacing, and self-sufficient repairs. Unlike recreational riding, you'll bike for 2-8+ hours with multi-sport fatigue, team coordination requirements, and constant route decisions.

<br/>

## Choosing the Right Bike

**Cross-Country (XC) Mountain Bike** ✅ Recommended for beginners: lightweight, front suspension (100-120mm), efficient for all-day riding.

**Hardtail vs. Full-Suspension**: Hardtails (front suspension only) are lighter, simpler to maintain, and more efficient on climbs. Full-suspension offers more comfort on rough terrain but is heavier.

**Bike Fit**: Saddle height should allow nearly straight legs at the bottom of the pedal stroke. Handlebars should be comfortable without straining. Test with full race gear. Frame size should allow 2-3 inches clearance over the top tube.

**Suspension Setup**: Adjust air pressure for your weight and terrain. Firmer for smooth trails and climbs; softer for technical, rocky trails.

<br/>

## Tire Choice and Setup

**Tire Width**: 2.1"–2.4" provides the best balance for adventure racing.

**Tread**: Low-profile knobs for dry/hardpack; tall, spaced knobs for mud/loose terrain. Medium tread works for mixed conditions.

**Tire Pressure**: Start at 20–26 psi front, 22–30 psi rear. Adjust for rider weight and terrain. Too high: harsh ride, less grip. Too low: pinch flats and sluggish feel.

**Tubeless Setup** ✅ Highly Recommended: Self-seals punctures, allows lower pressure for better grip, reduces flats. Requires tubeless-ready rims/tires, sealant (refresh every 2-3 months), and valve stems. Carry spare tubes and plug kit as backup.

<br/>

## Essential Repair Kit

**Mandatory Items**:
- 1-2 spare tubes (even with tubeless)
- Tire levers (2-3)
- Pump or CO₂ inflator with backup
- Multi-tool with Allen keys, chain breaker, screwdrivers
- Quick links (2-3 spare)
- Tubeless plug kit (if tubeless)
- Patch kit

**Highly Recommended**:
- Spare derailleur hanger (bike-specific, very fragile)
- Duct tape (wrap on seat post)
- Zip ties (5-10)
- Small bottle of chain lube

Store in saddle bag or frame bag. Test all tools during training rides.

<br/>

## Common Mechanical Issues and Fixes

**Flat Tires**: With tubeless, remove sharp objects, use plug kit, re-inflate. If unsuccessful, install tube. With tubes, remove wheel, use tire levers, replace/patch tube, check tire interior for debris, reinstall.

**Dropped Chain**: Lift chain back onto chainring. Prevention: shift to easier gears before stopping.

**Derailleur Issues**: Adjust barrel adjuster for poor shifting, check cable tension. Bent hanger may need careful straightening or replacement.

**Broken Chain**: Use chain breaker to remove broken link, reconnect with quick link. Practice this repair before race day.

**Brake Problems**: Squealing is usually cosmetic. Weak braking: check pad wear, adjust cable tension. Rubbing: adjust calipers or check wheel trueness.

<br/>

## Pacing Strategy

Adventure races are endurance events. Maintain conversational pace for hours, save energy for later disciplines, and walk steep/technical climbs when strategic.

**Avoid the "bike high"**: Resist going too hard after trekking sections. Monitor heart rate (stay in zone 2-3). Walking steep climbs may be faster and save energy.

**Nutrition and Hydration**: Eat every 20-30 minutes, drink frequently. Don't wait for hunger or thirst signals. See our [Nutrition Guide](/content/nutrition.md) for details.

<br/>

## Team Riding Dynamics

**Staying Together**: Most races require teams within sight (often 100m). Regroup frequently at intersections and hills. Call out stops. Ride single-file on singletrack.

**Drafting**: On wider trails, rotate front position every 2-5 minutes. Communicate rotation changes. Drafting saves 20-30% effort for following riders.

**Supporting Slower Riders**: Stronger riders can carry extra gear, provide encouragement, give gentle pushes on climbs, and check in frequently.

**Navigation**: Assign a primary navigator. Stop for complex route decisions. Call out turns in advance. Follow obvious features when possible. See our [Navigation Guide](/content/navigation.md).

<br/>

## Training for Adventure Racing Mountain Biking

**Build Endurance**: Long rides (2-4+ hours) at conversational pace. Back-to-back days to simulate multi-leg racing. Mixed terrain practice.

**Develop Skills**: Cornering (lean bike, look ahead), climbing (shift early, stay seated), descending (weight back, look ahead), technical features (roots, rocks, drops).

**Practice Navigation**: Combine rides with map-reading. Navigate to specific points in unfamiliar areas. Practice checking maps while riding safely.

**Mechanical Skills**: Learn to fix flats, use your multi-tool, repair chains, and adjust derailleurs before race day.

**Team Training**: Ride together to learn pacing and strengths. Practice communication and test race gear.

<br/>

## Race Day Bike Tips

**Pre-Race**: Inspect tires, brakes, chain, and bolts. Test all gears. Pre-ride course section if allowed. Verify complete repair kit.

**During the Race**: Start conservatively. Watch for hazards (roots, rocks, water crossings). Communicate constantly about obstacles, turns, and stops. Handle mechanicals calmly and methodically.

**Bike Security**: When leaving bikes to reach checkpoints, lock or hide them if allowed, remember exact location, and take navigation tools.

<br/>

## Common Beginner Mistakes

- Riding too hard early—save energy for the full race
- Inadequate repair kit—missing key tools costs hours
- Poor bike fit—causes discomfort over long distances
- Not practicing repairs—know how to fix problems
- Ignoring nutrition—bonking is preventable
- Separating from teammates—stay together
- Wrong gear for terrain—shift before hills
- Skipping technical trail training—road riding doesn't prepare you

<br/>

## Summary

Mountain biking in adventure racing is about efficiency, reliability, and teamwork—not speed. By choosing the right bike, setting it up properly, carrying essential repair tools, pacing yourself wisely, and working well with your team, you'll maximize your performance and enjoyment on the bike legs.

Remember: the fastest bike leg is the one where you don't have a mechanical, don't get separated from your team, and still have energy for the next discipline. Ride smart, stay together, and keep moving forward.
