# Mountain Biking in Adventure Racing

Bike legs reward teams that are **steady**, **organized**, and **mechanically calm**. You’re not trying to win a Strava segment—you’re trying to cover distance, navigate, and arrive at the next transition with your team intact.

## What biking looks like in races

- Terrain can include gravel roads, doubletrack, singletrack, and occasional hike-a-bike.
- Navigation happens on the bike: quick map checks and frequent route decisions.
- Teams usually must stay together (race-specific separation rules vary).
- You’re self-supported for repairs and problem-solving.

Related: [Navigation](/content/navigation.md), [Team Dynamics](/content/team-dynamics.md)

## A simple bike setup that works

### Bike choice

- A reliable XC or trail mountain bike is ideal.
- Hardtails are lighter and simpler; full-suspension is more comfortable on rough courses.

### Tires (keep it boring)

- Choose a versatile tread you trust in wet and dry.
- If you run tubeless, top up sealant and still carry a tube.

> [!TIP]
> The best tire choice is the one you’ve trained on and won’t think about all day.

### Fit and comfort

- Fix obvious fit problems early (saddle height, reach, hand numbness).
- Test your setup with your race pack on.

## The repair kit you actually need

Carry what lets you solve the common failures quickly:

- Tube (even if tubeless) + tire levers
- Pump or CO₂ (plus a backup plan)
- Multi-tool that fits your bike + chain tool
- Quick link
- Tubeless plug kit (if tubeless)

Nice-to-have if your race is long/remote:

- Spare derailleur hanger
- Small tape + a few zip ties

## Common problems (and the fast response)

- **Flat**: plug/seal or swap a tube; move on.
- **Dropped chain**: put it back on; wash hands later.
- **Broken chain**: remove the bad link; reconnect with a quick link.
- **Shifting weird**: check for bent derailleur/hanger; do small barrel-adjust tweaks.

Practice at home: fix a flat and install a quick link without thinking.

## Pacing and team flow

- Ride at a pace you can hold for hours; save matches for when it matters.
- Regroup at natural decision points (intersections, hilltops, obvious features).
- Call hazards and stops early: “stopping,” “left in 20,” “rock garden.”
- Eat and drink steadily; it’s easier on the bike than on foot.

Related: [Nutrition & Hydration](/content/nutrition.md)

## Race-day checklist

Before the start:

- Tires/brakes/shifting OK
- Bolts snug (especially stem/handlebar)
- Repair kit complete and reachable

During the leg:

- Stay together and communicate
- Fix mechanicals methodically (panic wastes time)
- If you stash bikes for an off-trail checkpoint, note the exact spot and take nav tools

## Common beginner mistakes

- Starting too hard because biking “feels easy”
- Not carrying the tools to fix the predictable failures
- Treating the bike leg as separate from the rest of the race (nutrition and pacing still matter)
