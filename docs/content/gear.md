# Adventure Racing Gear Guide

Gear should do three things: **keep you safe**, **keep you moving**, and **reduce preventable problems** (cold, blisters, mechanicals). This page stays high-level and race-oriented.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The race director’s mandatory gear list wins over everything here.

## How to think about gear

- **Mandatory**: required for safety and self-sufficiency.
- **Essential**: not always mandatory, but commonly critical (e.g., foot care, basic bike repair).
- **Nice-to-have**: comfort/performance items that should earn their weight.

## Mandatory gear (typical categories)

Mandatory gear varies by race, but it often includes:

- **Emergency/safety**: whistle, emergency blanket, first aid basics, small knife/multi-tool.
- **Light**: headlamp + spare power (even in “day races” if delays happen).
- **Communication**: phone (usually for emergencies only) + waterproofing.
- **Water safety** (if paddling): PFD (and helmet for whitewater).
- **Navigation**: compass, map case, waterproof marker/pen.

For navigation techniques, see [Navigation](/content/navigation.md).

## Clothing and footwear (what matters most)

- **Avoid cotton**. Favor synthetic or merino.
- **Plan for wet + wind**: a light waterproof layer is commonly required.
- **Footwear is the highest-impact decision**: broken-in shoes, good traction, and socks you trust.
- **Bring at least one spare sock option** for longer events or wet conditions.

More detail: [Trekking](/content/trekking.md).

## Discipline essentials

### Biking (if included)

- Helmet
- A bike that shifts/brakes reliably
- Minimum repair kit: tube(s), tire levers, pump/CO₂, multi-tool, quick link

More detail: [Biking](/content/biking.md).

### Paddling (if included)

- PFD (often provided; personal fit is better)
- Dry storage for critical items (phone, layers, food)

More detail: [Paddling](/content/paddling.md).

## Packs, food, and water (keep it simple)

- Choose a carry system that holds required safety gear plus water and snacks.
- Make food **easy to access while moving**.
- Waterproof critical items (phone, headlamp, emergency layer).

More detail: [Nutrition & Hydration](/content/nutrition.md).

## Transition organization (the only rule)

Transitions are mostly about not losing time to chaos:

- Pack “next leg” items together.
- Put the first-needed items on top.
- Use a short checklist so nothing is forgotten.

More detail: [Transition Areas](/content/transition-areas.md).

## Common beginner mistakes (and the fix)

- **Overpacking**: carry what’s required + what you will actually use.
- **Untested gear**: no new shoes, lights, or packs on race day.
- **Bad waterproofing**: assume you’ll get wet.
- **Ignoring foot care**: treat hot spots early.
- **Skipping the gear list**: missing mandatory items can end your race.

## Quick checklists

### Sprint (2–4 hours)

- Safety: whistle, emergency blanket, basic first aid, small knife/multi-tool, phone (waterproofed)
- Light: headlamp + spare power
- Nav: compass, map case, waterproof marker/pen
- Clothing: weather-appropriate layers + rain shell if required
- Food/water: enough for 4–6 hours (buffer for mistakes)
- If biking: helmet + basic repair kit
- If paddling: PFD + dry storage

### 12-hour (additions)

- Backup light/power strategy
- Warmer layer + gloves if conditions warrant
- Water purification if required
- More food (real calories, not just gels)

### 24+ hour (additions)

- More robust warmth and battery strategy
- More complete repair plan for bikes
- A clearer transition/bin system
