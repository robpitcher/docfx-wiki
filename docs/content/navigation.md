# Navigation in Adventure Racing

Navigation is a core adventure racing skill: it’s how you choose efficient routes, find checkpoints, and avoid turning a good day into a long day. This page keeps things conceptual and race-oriented (not a map-and-compass textbook).

## What “navigation” means in a race

In practice, navigation is a repeatable loop:

1. **Plan a leg** from where you are to where you need to go.
2. **Execute the plan** while staying in contact with the map.
3. **Confirm** you’re where you think you are (often).
4. **Adjust** quickly when reality doesn’t match the plan.

## The principles that win time

- **Stay in map contact**: frequent small checks beat rare big checks.
- **Prefer simple, confirmable routes** when tired, cold, or behind schedule.
- **Use handrails**: linear features that guide you (trails, streams, ridgelines, roads).
- **Use an attack point**: a clear feature near the checkpoint to “reset certainty” before the final approach.
- **Use a catching feature**: a big feature beyond the checkpoint so you know you’ve gone too far.
- **Slow down near the end**: most wasted time happens in the last few hundred meters.

> [!TIP]
> If you’re debating whether to stop and verify your location, stop early. Small hesitation is usually the first sign you’re about to lose time.

## Route choice (high level)

Most route choices are a trade between:

- **Direct**: shorter distance, more uncertainty and terrain risk.
- **Indirect**: longer distance, faster movement and easier confirmation.

Ask these questions quickly:

- What terrain is likely to be slow (vegetation, steep climbs, wetlands)?
- Where are the easiest places to confirm location (junctions, distinct bends, ridge/valley shapes)?
- What’s the team’s current state (fatigue, daylight, morale)?

## Executing a leg (a simple workflow)

1. **Pick your “spine”** (handrail) and decide where you’ll leave it.
2. **Pick an attack point** close to the checkpoint.
3. **Choose a catching feature** beyond the checkpoint.
4. **Move and confirm**: call out features as you pass them.
5. **Final approach**: slow down, compare map-to-terrain, and verify before leaving.

## When you’re lost (relocation checklist)

- **Stop** and prevent “drifting” into a bigger mistake.
- **Get oriented** (what direction have you been moving, what terrain are you on?).
- **Work backward** to the last point you were certain.
- **Look for a catching feature** you can deliberately navigate to (road, large stream, major ridge).
- **Reset the plan** once you’re back on a known feature.

## Team navigation (keep it lightweight)

- **One person drives, one person checks**: a primary navigator plus a sanity-checker.
- **Rotate** on long races to avoid mental fatigue.
- **Speak up early**: uncertainty shared early is cheap; uncertainty shared late is expensive.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Waiting too long to confirm location.
- Overcommitting to a plan after the terrain proves it wrong.
- Skipping attack points and “hoping” the checkpoint appears.
- Searching widely instead of resetting to a known feature.
- Turning navigation into blame instead of a team problem to solve.
