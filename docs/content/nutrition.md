# Nutrition and Hydration for Adventure Racing

Adventure racing punishes “set it and forget it” fueling. The goal is simple: **steady energy, steady fluids, and a stomach that keeps working**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is general guidance, not medical advice. If you have medical conditions or heat illness risk, consult a clinician and follow your race’s safety guidance.

## The 3 rules that cover most races

1. **Start early**: fuel within the first hour.
2. **Go small and frequent**: a bite/sip every 20–30 minutes.
3. **Avoid surprises**: don’t introduce new foods on race day.

## Simple targets

- **Carbs per hour**:
  - Sprint (2–4h): ~30–40 g/hour
  - 8–12h: ~40–60 g/hour
  - 24h+: ~50–70 g/hour (if tolerated)
- **Fluids**: drink to conditions (heat, intensity, sweat rate). If you’re peeing occasionally and thinking clearly, you’re usually in a workable zone.
- **Electrolytes**: use salty foods/drinks on longer races and in heat.

If you only remember one thing: **slower and fueled beats fast and empty**.

## Pre-race (keep it boring)

- 24–48 hours: eat normal meals with extra carbs if you tolerate them.
- Race morning (2–3 hours before): familiar meal with carbs + a bit of protein.
- Final hour: top off with small, easy carbs if needed.

## What to eat during the race

Use a mix of:

- **Sport fuel** (gels/chews/drink mix): convenient, easy to measure.
- **Real food**: variety and morale (especially after several hours).

Good “real food” patterns:

- Something salty + something sweet
- Soft foods you can eat while moving
- Options that still work when you’re cold, wet, or tired

## Transition areas: your best chance to reset

Use transitions to:

- Eat something more substantial (but keep it quick)
- Refill bottles/bladders
- Swap food variety to prevent flavor fatigue

For logistics and speed: [Transition Areas](/content/transition-areas.md).

## Hydration (high-level)

- Drink consistently rather than chugging rarely.
- In heat or long efforts, include electrolytes via drink mix, tablets, or salty foods.
- Avoid extremes: both dehydration and over-drinking can wreck your day.

## When your stomach starts to go sideways

Common pattern: intensity + heat + dehydration + too much sweetness.

Try this reset:

1. **Back off the effort** for 5–10 minutes.
2. **Sip fluids** (water or light electrolyte).
3. Switch to **bland carbs** and small amounts.
4. Add **salty** options if you’ve been taking only sweet fuel.

## Bonk prevention (the short version)

- Fuel early and keep a timer-like routine.
- If you feel the “fog” coming on, treat it as a fuel problem first.
- A full reset can take 20–30 minutes, so intervene early.

## Team coordination

- Agree on a simple schedule (e.g., “eat every 30 minutes”).
- Rotate who reminds the team.
- Carry a variety so someone can swap when their stomach rejects a food.

For teamwork systems: [Team Dynamics](/content/team-dynamics.md).

## Quick starter plans

### Sprint (2–4 hours)

- Eat small carbs regularly + drink steadily.
- Bring a little extra food in case navigation adds time.

### 8–12 hours

- Mix sport fuel and real food.
- Plan one “real” snack/mini-meal at a transition.
- Add electrolytes (especially in heat).

### 24+ hours

- Variety matters more than perfection.
- Have a plan for night/cold appetite changes.
- Expect to rely on “whatever you can keep down” late.
