# Paddling in Adventure Racing

<br/>

## Introduction

Paddling is often the most intimidating discipline for new adventure racers, combining physical endurance, technical skill, navigation, and teamwork on the water. Unlike recreational paddling, racing involves checkpoint navigation, time pressure, close team coordination, and portaging between water sections.

<br/>

## Types of Boats Used in Adventure Racing

### Canoes
Open-top boats for 2-3 people, using single-bladed paddles. Excellent for team coordination and gear storage, stable on flatwater. Less efficient in wind, heavier to portage. Best for sprint to 12-hour races.

### Kayaks
Enclosed boats with lower profile, using double-bladed paddles. More efficient in wind and waves, faster on open water, better tracking. Less gear space than canoes. Best for longer paddling sections and choppy conditions.

### Packrafts
Ultra-lightweight inflatable rafts (5-8 lbs vs. 40-80 lbs for traditional boats). Pack into backpack, extremely light, perfect for mixed terrain races. Slower and more affected by wind, require 5-10 minute inflation. Best for expedition races and multiple water crossings.

### Race-Provided Boats
Many races provide boats, eliminating ownership and transport needs. May be heavier or less maintained, but accessible for beginners.

<br/>

## Basic Paddling Technique

### Forward Stroke (Most Important)
1. Sit upright with good posture
2. Rotate torso as you reach forward
3. Plant paddle fully in water near feet
4. Pull toward hip using torso rotation
5. Exit cleanly at hip

**Key Point**: Power comes from torso rotation and core, not arms. Prevents fatigue and blisters.

### Other Essential Strokes
- **Sweep Stroke**: Wide arc from front to back for turning
- **Reverse Stroke**: Paddling backward to slow or maneuver
- **Draw Stroke**: Pull sideways to move boat laterally
- **Rudder Stroke** (canoes): Held at angle behind boat for steering

### Reading Water
- Current flows faster on outside of bends
- Eddies (calm pockets behind rocks) for resting
- Watch for rocks, logs, shallow areas
- Anticipate wind constantly

## Safety Essentials

### Mandatory Gear
**PFD**: Must be worn at all times. Choose paddling-specific for mobility.
**Helmet**: Required for whitewater or rapids.
**Whistle**: Attached to PFD (three blasts = distress).

### Additional Safety Items
Throw bag, spare paddle, knife/line cutter, waterproof first aid kit, repair kit (essential for packrafts).

### Clothing
**Dress for immersion—assume you'll get wet.**

**Warm Weather**: Quick-dry synthetics, sun protection, water shoes.
**Cold Weather**: Wetsuit/drysuit for water below 60°F, layered wool/synthetics, waterproof jacket, gloves, spare dry clothes.

### Capsize Protocol
1. Stay calm, hold paddle
2. Stay with boat unless dangerous
3. Get to shore/shallow water
4. Empty boat and re-enter or swim to shore
5. Check on teammates

**Prevention**: Keep weight low and centered, move slowly when shifting, lean into turns.

## Team Coordination in Boats

### Roles in Tandem Boats
**Bow (Front)**: Provides power and rhythm, handles navigation, calls obstacles, sets stroke rate.
**Stern (Back)**: Steers boat, matches bow rhythm, makes directional decisions. More experienced paddler sits here.

### Communication & Synchronization
- Agree on roles and strategy before launching
- Call out turns, obstacles, checkpoints
- Match cadence and switch sides together
- Share navigation and steering duties to prevent fatigue

### Navigation on Water
Use waterproof map case, compass more reliable than GPS, identify shore landmarks, plan route before launching. Learn more in our [Navigation Guide](/content/navigation.md).

<br/>

## Portaging (Carrying Your Boat)

Portaging means carrying boats and gear over land between water sections or around obstacles.

### Techniques
**Canoes/Kayaks**: Two-person carry (bow and stern), or overhead carry with yoke. Practice lifting techniques before race day.
**Packrafts**: Deflate, roll tightly, pack in backpack. Much faster than heavy boats.

### Gear Organization
Use dry bags, minimize items, keep map/snacks accessible, balance weight for better handling.

<br/>

## Training for Paddling

### Build Fitness & Skills
- Practice 1-2 hour sustained paddling sessions
- Take beginner lesson for proper technique
- Core and upper body strength work
- Master forward stroke, sweep, and rudder
- Practice capsize recovery in safe conditions

### Team Preparation
- Paddle together to build communication and rhythm
- Practice portaging and packing
- Navigate on water with map and compass
- Simulate race fatigue (paddle after biking/running)
- Train in challenging weather conditions

<br/>

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

1. **Underestimating paddling**: Practice before race day to build technique and endurance
2. **Poor stroke technique**: Take a lesson, use torso rotation not just arms
3. **Navigation struggles**: Use waterproof map case, practice landmark identification
4. **Skipping safety gear**: Always wear required PFD, helmet, and carry spare paddle
5. **Inadequate waterproofing**: Use quality dry bags, double-bag electronics
6. **Inefficient portaging**: Practice proper lifting form before racing
7. **Poor communication**: Talk constantly, establish roles before launching
8. **Ignoring weather**: Monitor wind, take breaks in lee of shore, adjust strategy
9. **Rushed transitions**: Take 30 seconds to organize, check gear before/after paddling

<br/>

## Paddling Strategy for Races

### Pre-Launch
Study map for checkpoints and hazards, agree on route, organize gear in dry bags, check boat and safety equipment.

### During Paddling
Start at sustainable pace, check navigation every 5-10 minutes, monitor teammates for fatigue/cold, fuel and hydrate, maintain visual contact in solo boats.

### Post-Paddle
Secure boat per race rules, change gear quickly, warm up immediately if cold.

<br/>

## Summary

Paddling requires physical fitness, technical skill, navigation, and teamwork. Choose the right boat, learn proper technique, prioritize safety, and practice with your team. The goal isn't expert paddling—it's moving safely and efficiently to checkpoints while staying together. Basic skills and a positive attitude ensure success.
