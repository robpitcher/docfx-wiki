# Paddling in Adventure Racing

Paddling legs mix endurance, navigation, and teamwork—often while you’re cold, wet, and making route decisions on moving water or in wind. The goal is simple: **move safely and efficiently between checkpoints**.

## Common boat setups

- **Canoes**: stable and roomy; tandem coordination matters.
- **Kayaks**: efficient in wind/waves; gear access can be tighter.
- **Packrafts**: light and portable; slower and more wind-affected.

Many races provide boats. Assume the race boats will be heavier and less “nice” than your personal gear.

## Safety first (non-negotiable)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Dress for immersion: assume you’ll get wet and plan for the water temperature and wind.

Typical requirements and best practices:

- **PFD**: worn on the water (and fitted so it won’t ride up).
- **Helmet**: often required for whitewater/rapids.
- **Whistle**: usually required; know the distress signal.
- Waterproof critical items (phone, headlamp/batteries, emergency layer).

If you capsize: stay calm, stay with the boat/paddle when safe, and get to shore to regroup.

## Technique (just enough)

- Sit tall, relax your grip, and use **torso rotation** instead of arm-only pulling.
- Aim for smooth, repeatable strokes; wild power surges waste energy.

If paddling is new to your team, a short lesson is worth more than a long article.

## Team coordination (tandem boats)

- Decide roles: **front sets rhythm**, **rear steers** (most of the time).
- Use simple calls: “hold,” “left,” “right,” “stop,” “easy.”
- If you switch sides together (canoes), do it consistently.

Related: [Team Dynamics](/content/team-dynamics.md)

## Navigation on water

- Keep the map readable (waterproof map case).
- Use shoreline features as handrails and checkpoints as “targets,” not vague directions.
- Make a plan before launching; stopping mid-water to debate costs time.

Related: [Navigation](/content/navigation.md)

## Portaging (carrying boats)

- Keep gear consolidated in a few dry bags.
- Lift as a team on a count; protect backs and shoulders.
- For packrafts, be fast at packing/rolling and securing straps.

## Race strategy (simple and repeatable)

Pre-launch:

- Agree on route and checkpoints
- Confirm safety gear and waterproofing
- Organize snacks/water so you can access them without chaos

On the water:

- Start easy; settle into a sustainable pace
- Confirm position regularly (especially in wind)
- Stay warm: keep moving, keep fueling

Post-paddle:

- Secure boat per race rules
- Change layers quickly if cold

## Common beginner mistakes

- Underestimating cold/wind and failing to waterproof critical items
- Poor communication in tandem boats
- Launching without a clear navigation plan
