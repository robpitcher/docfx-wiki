# Rules (Adventure Racing)

Adventure racing rules vary by event, but most races share the same core themes: **safety**, **fairness**, and **self-sufficiency**.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Your race packet and mandatory gear list are the source of truth. If this page conflicts with race-specific instructions, follow the race instructions.

## The big rules you should expect

### 1) Stay together

Most races require teams to remain together and within a defined distance (often called “team separation”). This usually means:

- You cannot split up to hunt checkpoints faster.
- If a teammate is struggling, the team must slow down and manage it together.

Related: [Team Dynamics](/content/team-dynamics.md)

### 2) Follow the course and the checkpoint order

Races may use:

- **Mandatory checkpoints** (must be completed)
- **Optional checkpoints** (score format)
- **Specific order** or **free order** (varies by format)

Related: [Race Formats](/content/race-formats.md)

### 3) Navigation rules (usually “no GPS for nav”)

Many races restrict GPS for navigation. Common patterns:

- GPS may be allowed only for emergency use (or not at all).
- Phones may be allowed but not for maps/navigation.
- Race-provided trackers are typically for safety and timing.

Related: [Navigation](/content/navigation.md)

### 4) Carry mandatory gear

Mandatory gear exists for safety and self-sufficiency. Typical expectations:

- You must have the required items **on you** (sometimes by leg, sometimes all race).
- Gear checks can happen at the start and in transitions.
- Missing gear commonly leads to time penalties or disqualification.

Related: [Gear](/content/gear.md)

### 5) Respect cutoffs and time limits

Races often include:

- **Leg cutoffs** (must leave a transition area by a certain time)
- **Course cutoffs** (must reach a checkpoint/TA by a certain time)
- **Overall time limit**

Cutoffs exist to keep teams safe and keep staff operations manageable.

## Penalties (typical)

Penalties vary by event, but common reasons include:

- Missing mandatory gear
- Skipping mandatory checkpoints
- Team separation
- Unsportsmanlike conduct
- Missing a timing/punch procedure

Some races allow “course shortening” options when teams are behind schedule; others require withdrawing. When in doubt, ask the race director.

## Safety expectations

### Medical and emergencies

Common safety rules:

- Help other teams in a true emergency.
- Notify staff if you encounter an injured racer.
- Follow race communication procedures (phone numbers, channels, check-in requirements).

### Environmental hazards

Be prepared for weather and terrain. Even short races can run long if navigation or mechanicals go wrong.

Related: [Nutrition & Hydration](/content/nutrition.md)

## Leave No Trace (and local regulations)

Expect rules around:

- Staying on permitted trails/areas (when required)
- Respecting private property boundaries
- Pack-in/pack-out trash
- Wildlife interactions (including food storage rules)

## Transitions and support

Rules vary widely, but common patterns include:

- Only certain areas allow access to your transition gear.
- Outside support may be restricted or prohibited.
- Transition procedures can be enforced (helmet rules, PFD rules, bike lights, etc.).

Related: [Transition Areas](/content/transition-areas.md)

## Sportsmanship

Adventure racing communities are small and supportive. Typical expectations:

- Be honest about mistakes (wrong checkpoint, missed punch, etc.).
- Treat volunteers and other teams with respect.
- Prioritize safety over competition.

---

If you’re new: focus on (1) staying together, (2) carrying the mandatory gear, and (3) meeting cutoffs. Everything else gets easier with experience.
