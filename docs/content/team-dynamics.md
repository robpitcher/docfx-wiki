# Team Dynamics in Adventure Racing

Adventure racing rewards teams that can keep moving while tired, cold, and occasionally wrong. Fitness matters—but **cohesion** is often the difference between finishing strong and unraveling.

## The one rule that drives everything

Most races require teams to stay together (exact distance varies by event). Even when rules are looser, the outcome is the same:

- The team is only as fast as its slowest member.
- Splitting up increases navigation errors and safety risk.

## Communication (keep it simple)

Good teams share short, useful updates:

- “Stop in 20 for a map check.”
- “Hot spot—need tape.”
- “Let’s eat now and push to the next feature.”

When noise or fatigue makes talking hard, pre-agree a few signals/phrases (stop, slow, regroup, emergency).

## Decision-making without drama

- Let a primary navigator propose the route.
- Let a second person confirm with a quick check.
- If you’re stuck, choose “good enough,” commit, and reassess at the next clear feature.

> [!TIP]
> Set a default tie-breaker before the race (e.g., “navigator decides on nav, captain decides on safety/withdrawal”).

Related: [Navigation](/content/navigation.md)

## Pacing and support

- Start conservatively and keep the slowest member out of the red.
- Use micro-breaks (20–60 seconds) instead of long collapses.
- Share the load: stronger members carry a bit more weight or handle more of the mental work.

Fueling is a team job—someone should be reminding everyone to eat and drink.

Related: [Nutrition & Hydration](/content/nutrition.md)

## Conflict and morale

Stress happens. The most effective pattern is:

1. Pause briefly.
2. Name the problem (cold, bonk, wrong turn, blister).
3. Fix the root cause.
4. Move on.

If morale drops, address basics first: calories, warmth, dry socks, a clear next milestone.

## Roles (rotate to share the load)

Common lightweight roles:

- Navigator / co-navigator
- Pacer (keeps team together)
- Food/water reminder
- “Sweeper” (stays last and watches for gaps)

Rotate roles at transitions or every hour so one person doesn’t burn out mentally.

Related: [Transition Areas](/content/transition-areas.md)

## Pre-race team agreement (10 minutes that saves hours)

- What’s our goal today (finish, podium, learn)?
- How do we make decisions under stress?
- What’s our plan for foot care, nutrition reminders, and navigation checks?
- What are our red lines for safety (hypothermia, injury, weather)?
