# Trekking in Adventure Racing

<br/>

## What is Trekking in Adventure Racing?

Trekking is the most demanding discipline in adventure racing, combining trail running, power hiking, bushwhacking, and off-trail navigation while carrying a pack for hours or days. Success requires fitness, smart pacing, navigation skills, proper footwear, foot care, and teamwork.

<br/>

## Trail Running vs. Race Trekking

### The Reality of Race Trekking

Adventure racing trekking involves mixed movement (running, hiking, sometimes crawling), power hiking on steep or technical terrain, variable conditions, and constant navigation. Even elite racers power hike—embrace it on inclines and rough terrain. Save running energy for flat or rolling terrain.

> [!TIP]
> Use trekking sections to rehearse your navigation chatter—call out bearings and landmarks even in training.

### Building Your Base
- Log time on feet with long, slow distance
- Train on varied terrain: rocks, roots, hills, technical features
- Carry weight (10-15 lbs) to simulate race conditions
- Practice navigation during training hikes

<br/>

## Bushwhacking and Off-Trail Navigation

Bushwhacking means traveling through unmarked wilderness—dense brush, boulder fields, or around obstacles like cliffs and swamps.

### Techniques
- Look for natural corridors: creek beds, ridgelines, clearings
- Use handrails (linear features) to maintain direction
- Spread out to find the best path, then call others to follow
- Be patient—detours around dense areas are often faster
- Wear long sleeves for protection

### Navigation While Trekking
- Check maps frequently, especially in featureless terrain
- Use terrain association to match visible features to your map
- Use dead reckoning on compass bearings across featureless areas
- Navigate to attack points (obvious features) near checkpoints
- If lost, identify surrounding features to relocate

Learn more in our [Navigation Guide](/content/navigation.md).

## Footwear and Foot Care

### Choosing Footwear

**Trail Running Shoes**: Lightweight, good drainage, less ankle support. Best for sprint races and experienced runners.

**Light Hiking Boots**: More ankle support and protection. Best for longer races, heavy packs, less experienced trekkers.

**Key Features**: Quick drainage, aggressive tread, snug heel with toe room, reinforced toe caps.

### Socks and Foot Care

**Socks**: Use synthetic or merino wool (not cotton). Consider double-layer socks. Pack 1-2 extra pairs.

**Before Racing**: Train in race shoes, toughen feet gradually, trim toenails a few days before.

**During Racing**:
- **Address hot spots immediately** with tape (Leukotape or athletic tape)
- Change socks at transitions
- Air out feet when possible
- Use lubrication (Vaseline, Body Glide) to reduce friction

**Blister Management**: Carry tape, moleskin, scissors. Drain large blisters (sterilize needle first) and cover with tape. Never ignore foot pain.

<br/>

## Pacing Strategy

Adventure racing is about sustainable, continuous forward movement for hours.

**Pacing Principles**:
- Start conservatively—easy to go too hard when fresh
- Find a rhythm you can maintain indefinitely
- Adjust for terrain: slower on climbs, efficient on flats
- Pace to the slowest team member

**Trekking Poles**: Reduce knee impact, help on climbs, improve balance. Can be cumbersome in thick brush—decide based on terrain.

<br/>

## Nutrition and Hydration While Trekking

**Fueling**: Eat small amounts every 20-30 minutes. Mix quick carbs (gels, chews) with longer-lasting foods (bars, nuts). Practice during training.

**Hydration**: Drink regularly. Carry 1-2 liters of water. Plan water sources and carry purification tablets or filter. Monitor urine color (clear to light yellow).

Learn more in our [Nutrition Guide](/content/nutrition.md).

## Night Trekking

Night trekking is significantly harder due to difficult navigation, fatigue peaks, and cold temperatures.

**Lighting**: Use high-quality headlamp (300+ lumens) with fresh batteries. Always carry backup or extra batteries.

**Navigation**: Check maps more frequently, confirm position using obvious features, take careful bearings, move slowly to avoid errors.

**Team Movement**: Stay close together (easier to lose teammates), use a sweeper to watch for stragglers, communicate frequently, plan regular rest breaks.

**Safety**: Watch for tripping hazards, use trekking poles for stability, take care on steep descents.

<br/>

## Moving Efficiently as a Team

**Core Principles**:
- **Stay together**: Most races require teams within 100 meters. Staying close improves safety, navigation, and morale.
- **Share the load**: Rotate navigation duties, stronger members carry extra weight, distribute tasks.
- **Use tow systems**: Bungee cord or rope helps lagging teammates without verbal pressure.
- **Minimize stops**: Navigate while moving, eat on the go, save longer stops for transitions.
- **Communicate constantly**: Share observations, call out obstacles, check on teammates, make group decisions.

**Building Team Skills**: Practice together, rotate roles, develop communication shortcuts, support each other mentally.

Learn more in our [Team Dynamics Guide](/content/team-dynamics.md).

<br/>

## Training for Trekking

**Build Endurance**: Long hikes/runs of 2-4+ hours, back-to-back training days, loaded carries (10-20 lbs).

**Develop Strength**: Hill repeats, core work, leg strength exercises (squats, lunges, step-ups).

**Practice Skills**: Off-trail movement on rough terrain, night training with headlamps, combined navigation practice.

**Simulate Race Conditions**: Train in varied weather, practice eating/drinking while moving, trek with your team.

<br/>

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Starting too fast**: Save energy for later when fatigue sets in
2. **Ignoring foot problems**: Hot spots become major blisters
3. **Poor nutrition**: Bonking is preventable with consistent fueling
4. **Over-relying on one navigator**: Spread the mental load
5. **Fighting terrain**: Detours are often faster than forcing through
6. **Not training specifically**: Road running doesn't prepare you for trails
7. **Neglecting night practice**: Night trekking is dramatically different

<br/>

## Summary

Trekking is the heart of adventure racing, combining navigation, endurance, and teamwork. Choose proper footwear, care for your feet, pace smartly, and move efficiently as a team. Remember: it's not about running every step—it's about continuous forward progress, staying together, and reaching checkpoints safely.
