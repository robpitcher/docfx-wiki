# Trekking in Adventure Racing

Trekking is where adventure racing really happens: **moving on foot while navigating**—often off-trail, in bad weather, and with a team that must stay together.

## What “trekking” includes

- Trail running where it’s efficient
- Power hiking on climbs and rough terrain
- Bushwhacking/off-trail travel to reach checkpoints
- Frequent map checks and micro route decisions

Related: [Navigation](/content/navigation.md)

## Pacing: aim for “all-day forward”

- Start easier than you think; the race is usually longer than planned.
- Hike early on steep grades instead of spiking effort.
- Keep stops short and purposeful (map check, foot care, snack).

Related: [Nutrition & Hydration](/content/nutrition.md)

## Off-trail travel (bushwhacking)

- Look for easier corridors (ridges, open forest, trails you can rejoin).
- Use handrails and attack points to reduce “wandering.”
- If progress is terrible, consider a longer-but-faster detour.

## Feet: the highest-risk system

Foot problems are one of the most common reasons teams slow down or drop.

- Avoid cotton; use socks you’ve tested.
- Choose shoes/boots you can wear wet for hours.
- Treat hot spots immediately (tape early beats blister care later).
- Use transitions to reset: dry socks, quick inspection.

Related: [Gear](/content/gear.md), [Transition Areas](/content/transition-areas.md)

## Night trekking essentials

- Use a reliable headlamp and have a spare power plan.
- Navigate more conservatively: confirm position often and slow down before errors compound.
- Stay tighter as a team; it’s easy to lose people in darkness.

## Team movement (the rule behind the rules)

- Stay together and pace to the slowest member.
- Rotate mental load (navigation checks, time checks, fueling reminders).
- Use simple communication: “stop,” “bearing,” “handrail,” “relocate.”

Related: [Team Dynamics](/content/team-dynamics.md)

## Training that transfers

- Time on feet on mixed terrain
- Practice navigation while tired
- Do one or two night sessions before a longer race

## Common mistakes

- Starting too fast (especially when you feel great)
- Ignoring hot spots until they become blisters
- Letting one person do all navigation for hours
